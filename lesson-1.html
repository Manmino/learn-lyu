Manmino Translated

A: Hello!
B: Hello! What’s your name?
A: My name is Lyu. And you?
B: I’m Makasa.
A: What is your age?
B: I am 21 years old.

Manmino Grammar Explained

In Manmino, the usual greeting is "Ni penan-ka", meaning 'Are you well?'. "Ni" means you. The usual response is "A penan" or just "Penan", which would be '(I am) well'.

  • Five second Question: Consider your own language. What is the most common way to greet someone, and what does it mean?

Manmino is a simple language. You have base words, and you optionally mark what a word is doing in a sentence by adding particles. For example, adding "-ji" (from the Chinese particle 之) to "A" or "Ni" makes it "A-ji" or "Ni-ji", going from "I" or "You" to "My" or "Your". Quite simple, isn't it? Another example: adding "-ka" (from the Japanese particle か and Korean 까) to a verb marks that the sentence is a question. In this case, the verb is "cey", which means 'to be', as in "I am", or "You are", or "He is", etc.

  • Check on learning: Let's take an example sentence "A waygyaynin cey": What do you think it means? ("Waygyaynin" means 'alien'.)

Now that we have words, what order do we place them in? In Manmino, there is no particular order in which a word goes, but with a catch; related words must be next to each other. This rule may sound difficult, but it really isn't. Let's break it down with a few examples.

Let's first consider the sentence "A cey Makasa". "A" means 'I', "cey" means 'is', and "Makasa" is a name. Therefore, "I cey Makasa" means 'I is Makasa' or 'I am Makasa'.

Now, let's consider the sentence "A nisipit-syey cey". We already know "A" means 'I', "cey" means 'is', and "Nisipit-syey" is '21 years (of age)'. So this sentence is 'I 21 years (of age) is', or 'I 21 years (of age) am'.

Although they're both sentences describing what kind of a person someone is, the first one placed the verb "cey" before the object "Makasa", while the second one placed it after the object "nisipit-syey". Is there a reason for this? NO! In Manmino, word order is flexible! As long as it makes sense what you are talking about, you can feel free to not worry too much about what order you place words in. Later we'll see some of the mechanisms Manmino uses to help people keep everything straight, but that's for future lessons. Just know this: "Don't worry about word orders too much".
